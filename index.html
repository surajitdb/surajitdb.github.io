Dr Surajit Deb Barma

Assistant Professor (Selection Grade), Department of Computer Science and Engineering, SMVITM, Bantakal, Karnataka

I build geospatial machine learning models for floods, rivers and groundwater, and I make them report how uncertain they are.

My work combines satellite and gridded climate data, Google Earth Engine pipelines and conformal prediction, mostly for the monsoon-driven catchments of Coastal Karnataka. My PhD at NITK Surathkal evaluated the water budget of the Brahmaputra River basin from satellite data. I have taught and researched since 2005 at institutions in India and at the University of Auckland, and I now teach AI and machine learning to undergraduate engineers.

Research

Four lines of work, all aimed at water decisions in data-sparse, monsoon-driven regions.

Flood susceptibility with uncertainty

Mapping where floods are likely along India's west coast with Google Earth Engine and machine learning ensembles, and testing whether the maps and their uncertainty estimates still hold when moved to another district.

Forecasting water under uncertainty

Streamflow, groundwater level and drought forecasting with machine learning and deep learning models, paired with conformal prediction intervals designed to keep their coverage when the monsoon changes regime.

Hydroclimatic trends and extremes

Rainfall trend analysis with innovative trend analysis (ITA), copula models of drought and joint hydroclimatic variables, and evaluation of satellite precipitation products such as ERA5, IMERG and CHIRPS.

GeoAI systems

Retrieval-augmented language model systems that answer questions about maps, and foundation models for climate, water and agriculture.

Forecasts that say how sure they are

A flood map or a streamflow forecast helps planners only if it also says how wrong it might be. Much of my work wraps machine learning models in conformal prediction, which turns past errors into intervals with a stated coverage, such as 90%.

The monsoon makes this hard. Flow regimes change abruptly at onset and withdrawal, so intervals have to widen and recover quickly. That is the focus of my current streamflow and groundwater work.
